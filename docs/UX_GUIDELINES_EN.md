# UX Guidelines for Knowledge Graph

When designing interfaces, follow these principles.

## Shneiderman's 8 Golden Rules
1. **Strive for consistency** — similar actions should produce similar results.
2. **Enable frequent users to use shortcuts** — keyboard shortcuts, quick access to frequent actions.
3. **Offer informative feedback** — every user action should have a visible response.
4. **Design dialogs to yield closure** — clear start, middle, and end to sequences.
5. **Offer error prevention and simple error handling** — confirm dangerous actions (deletion), block invalid input.
6. **Permit easy reversal of actions** — undo/redo capability, exit from any mode.
7. **Support internal locus of control** — give users control and make them feel in charge.
8. **Reduce short-term memory load** — don't make users remember information from one window to another.

## Nielsen's 10 Heuristics
1. **Visibility of system status** — always show what's happening (loading, processing, result).
2. **Match between system and the real world** — use familiar terms ("star", "link"), avoid technical jargon.
3. **User control and freedom** — "Cancel", "Back" buttons, action confirmation.
4. **Consistency and standards** — consistent rules across all pages (e.g., "Save" button always at top).
5. **Error prevention** — block actions that could lead to data loss.
6. **Recognition rather than recall** — visible elements (menus, hints) instead of requiring users to remember commands.
7. **Flexibility and efficiency of use** — keyboard shortcuts, search, filters for experienced users.
8. **Aesthetic and minimalist design** — don't overload the screen, leave whitespace.
9. **Help users recognize, diagnose, and recover from errors** — clear messages, not "404".
10. **Help and documentation** — short hints, links to documentation.

## Dieter Rams' 10 Principles
1. **Good design is innovative** — new features, not just decorations.
2. **Good design makes a product useful** — the product should help the user.
3. **Good design is aesthetic** — beautiful design enhances usability.
4. **Good design makes a product understandable** — the interface explains itself.
5. **Good design is unobtrusive** — the tool doesn't distract from the task.
6. **Good design is honest** — don't exaggerate capabilities.
7. **Good design is long-lasting** — design doesn't become outdated quickly.
8. **Good design is thorough down to the last detail** — every detail is thought through.
9. **Good design is environmentally friendly** — efficient use of resources (memory, battery).
10. **Good design is as little design as possible** — less, but better.

## Interface Checklist
- [ ] Do all actions have feedback?
- [ ] Can dangerous actions be undone?
- [ ] Are there keyboard shortcuts for frequent operations?
- [ ] Are icons and button labels clear?
- [ ] Is the screen not overloaded?
- [ ] Do colors and fonts meet contrast requirements?
- [ ] Are there hints when hovering over unclear elements?
